# ASET — Research & trend brief
## Global equity correction / AI infrastructure bubble question
**Prepared:** 22 November 2026 (Asia/Kolkata) | **Market data window:** latest US close Friday, 20 November 2026

## Editorial thesis

Do not say “the AI bubble has burst” as a fact. The evidence supports a sharper, defensible question: **markets are in a correction, and investors are repricing whether AI infrastructure spending will generate enough durable cash flow.** The strongest differentiator from generic crash explainers is the ECB-documented financing network across chip makers, cloud providers, AI labs, lenders and long-term commitments.

## Verified market snapshot

- Reuters reported on 22 Nov 2026 that the S&P 500 was 11.6% below its 6 Oct record after a seven-session decline; the Nasdaq had erased its 2026 gains.
- AP's 20 Nov close report gives the daily and weekly moves: S&P 500 −3.7% Friday and −8.6% for the week; Nasdaq −5.3% Friday and −10.0% for the week. AP reports the S&P close at 6,910.39 and Nasdaq at 22,617.73.
- Reuters' 20 Nov global markets wrap reports Taiwan equities −17% for the week, the steepest weekly fall since the early 1990s.

## Primary research: ECB November 2026

The ECB Financial Stability Review box “The debt financing of AI and its implications for financial stability” says:

- Big Tech's total off-balance-sheet commitments were estimated at more than USD 4 trillion as of Q2 2026.
- These are future contractual commitments that are debt-like economically, but are not recognised as current liabilities on hyperscaler balance sheets under US accounting rules. **Do not label the $4T figure “debt” or “hidden debt”.**
- The ECB describes circular links: cloud service providers and AI labs buy/lease chips, sometimes financed by chip makers; future infrastructure commitments can support borrowing by asset owners; hyperscalers are also linked by lending and equity stakes.
- The ECB says free cash flows turned negative for most US hyperscalers in 2026 as AI capex exceeded operating cash flow. It also notes Broadcom and Nvidia free cash flows rose on strong chip demand.
- The ECB does not say AI is fake or that a crash is certain. It says AI can transform the economy, while debt, circular financing, overcapacity and misallocated capital could create losses if monetisation disappoints.

## Terminology guardrail

Fidelity's 6 Apr 2026 investor education article describes a correction as generally at least a 10% decline from a recent high and a bear market as typically a 20% or greater fall. These are market conventions, not a legally fixed definition. The S&P's 11.6% drawdown therefore fits correction territory, not a confirmed bear market.

## Cross-check / caveats

1. Market index levels are snapshots, not live prices; US markets were closed on 22 Nov (Sunday), so the latest close used is 20 Nov.
2. AP and Reuters agree on the 20 Nov daily declines and Nasdaq's weekly fall; Reuters supplies the 11.6% peak drawdown and Taiwan weekly decline.
3. The $4T number is an estimate of off-balance-sheet commitments at Q2 2026, not the same as recognised debt or a cash loss.
4. Circular financing is a risk mechanism, not proof of fraud.
5. A genuine AI productivity boom can coexist with overvalued stocks and risky financing.
6. This is educational market analysis, not investment, tax or trading advice.
