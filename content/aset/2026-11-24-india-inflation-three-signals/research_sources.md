## Definitions and interpretation guardrails

1. CPI is a household-consumption price measure. It is not a personal inflation rate for every household.
2. WPI measures wholesale-stage goods prices and has a different basket and weights from CPI. Do not describe WPI as the rate at which all shop prices rose.
3. The Core Industries index covers eight sectors; it is not total industrial production, total GDP or listed-company earnings.
4. October CPI, WPI and ICI readings are provisional. Keep that label in on-screen graphics and metadata.
5. CPI uses base year 2024=100. Do not join it mechanically to the earlier 2012=100 series.
6. The WPI release reports a weighted response rate of 81.1% for the provisional October index. This is a compilation caveat, not a claim that the index is 81.1% accurate.
7. Rising input costs do not automatically translate one-for-one into retail prices or lower corporate margins. Pricing power, demand, contracts, inventories, productivity and competition matter.
8. The repo-rate decision on 7 October is historical. Do not imply a future hike is certain. The December MPC meeting is scheduled, not a predetermined policy action.
9. Do not attribute the 23 November Indian equity-market decline to the October inflation releases without evidence.
10. No individual security, target price, trade signal or personalized financial advice appears in either script.
