Editorial question: How can US real GDP accelerate to 4.3% annualised while October PCE inflation remains 3.8% year-on-year? What does this mean for the Federal Reserve, bond yields and global listed companies?

Core facts:
- BEA second estimate: Q3 2026 real GDP +4.3% annualised; Q2 +2.2%.
- October PCE price index: +0.3% month-on-month; +3.8% year-on-year. Core PCE: +0.3% m/m and +3.3% y/y.
- October real PCE was unchanged month-on-month; nominal PCE +0.3%.
- October 27–28 FOMC minutes: target range held at 3.75%–4.00%; this is historical meeting context, not a prediction.

Caveats: GDP is seasonally adjusted annualised quarterly growth. PCE inflation is a different measure and period. No inference that one quarter proves durable strength or that inflation guarantees a rate increase.
