# ASET Research Brief — 26 November 2026

## Selected angle
US Section 301 determinations and proposed trade actions affecting 14 economies, including India: what is decided, what is only proposed, and how listed-company earnings could be exposed.

## Primary source
- USTR, 25 November 2026, “USTR Makes Determinations and Proposes Actions in 14 Section 301 Investigations Relating to Structural Excess Capacity and Production in Manufacturing Sectors.” https://www.ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural
- Geography: United States and 14 investigated economies: China, EU, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan and India.
- USTR says it made Section 301 determinations for 14 economies. For India, it proposes tariffs of up to 25% on goods, net of existing Section 301 duties, with exemptions; it says it may consider 17% if India reaches a qualifying Agreement on Reciprocal Trade responsive to the findings. This is a proposal, not a statement that a new 25% tariff is already in force.
- USTR says the investigations began 11 March 2026, with public comments and hearings; its November 25 release publishes economy-specific reports and proposed actions.

## Analytical framework (interpretation, not reported fact)
Potential company channels include direct US export exposure, imported intermediate inputs, supplier substitution, customer demand, pricing power, inventory timing, FX, working capital and capital expenditure. The impact is company- and product-specific; tariff incidence may be shared among importer, exporter, supplier and customer. A proposed tariff rate is not a forecast of the effective duty paid by every company.

## Limitations
The USTR press release is the primary source for the administration's position, not an independent adjudication of the underlying claims. This brief does not independently model HS-code coverage, exemptions, origin rules, customs valuation, or the eventual Federal Register implementation. No company-level earnings estimate is made.
